    Help us test the MS-50G+

    The MS-50G+ pages of this site are built from the official ZOOM documents and from the file format of the MS-60B+, which has been checked on a real pedal. Nobody has tried a patch made here on a real MS-50G+ yet. If you own one, a few short tests done at home would tell us what is right and what needs fixing.

    You do not need to know how the files work: every test says what to do and what to send back. Do one test or all of them, every answer helps, and you can stop whenever you like.

    The short version: before you start and tests 1 to 3, about 50 minutes. Everything together is about 4 hours. The times are estimates for someone who has not used the app much; the later tests go faster once you know the steps.

    Before you start

    You need

    • The MS-50G+, switched on, and an iPhone or iPad with the free Handy Guitar Lab for MS-50G+ app (App Store), connected to the pedal with a USB data cable. The Handy Guitar Lab guide (PDF) says which cable and adapter your device needs.
    • A computer, or an iPad held sideways, to open this site and download the test patches, and a way to move a file to the iPhone or iPad (AirDrop, Mail or Files).
    • An email address to send the results from (see the end of the page).

    Look after your patches

    • Copying a patch to the pedal overwrites that memory. Make a backup of all the memories first (the back-up pages of the Handy Guitar Lab guide show how, and how to restore it), and use free memories for the tests.
    • Do not turn the pedal's knobs while the app is connected. On the MS-60B+ the pedal saved by itself what had been touched.

    Write down

    • The firmware version of the pedal: on the pedal, select VERSION on the MENU screen.
    • The version of the Handy Guitar Lab app, and the model and system version of your iPhone or iPad.

    Test 1: play a generated patch

    About 15 minutes. This checks that a patch made on this site is accepted by the app and by the pedal, and that the values arrive as they were set. The values are unusual on purpose, so that a value that ends up in the wrong place is easy to see.

    1. Open the TEST A patch in the Patch builder. It holds a compressor, an amp and a delay, with these values:
      EffectValues
      COMPRESSORSENSE 6, ATTCK FAST, TONE 5, VOL 50
      MS1959 DRIVEIN1 30, IN2 70, VOL 60, BASS 40, MID 55, TREBLE 65, PRESENCE 45
      DELAYTIME 350, MODE LONG, REPEAT 30, MIX 25
    2. Note the DSP load the builder shows (about 39.6%), then press Download .zptc.
    3. Move the file to your iPhone or iPad.
    4. Load it on the pedal as described in How to load it on the pedal: import it in the Pool, then copy it to a free memory.
    5. Select that memory on the pedal, look at the effects and their values, and play a little.

    Send us the answers to these questions

    • Did the app import the file? If it showed a message, write it down as it appears.
    • Are the three effects there, in this order, with the values above?
    • Does the pedal show the same effects and values? Write down any value that is different.
    • Which processor or DSP percentage does the app show for the patch?
    • Does it sound as it should (no silence, noise or clicks)?

    Screenshots of the app and a photo of the pedal's display are very welcome.

    Test 2: share a patch made in the app

    About 10 minutes. This shows us how the app writes a patch. Nothing from this site is involved.

    1. In the app, pick a free memory and add one COMPRESSOR. Leave its values as they are, name the patch TEST COMP and save it.
    2. Write down the four values the app shows for the compressor (SENSE, ATTCK, TONE and VOL).
    3. On the Patch Memory screen tap Select, tick that memory, tap the Share button and send the file to yourself (Mail or Files). The Handy Guitar Lab guide shows the steps under "Sharing a Patch Memory".

    Send us the .zptc file and the four values.

    Test 3: six effects in one patch

    About 15 minutes. On the MS-60B+ a six-effect patch written over USB lost its last effect on the pedal, because the pedal holds six entries. We need to know whether all six effects of a patch from the builder reach the MS-50G+ through the app.

    1. Open the TEST C patch in the Patch builder. It holds six effects: COMPRESSOR, TS DRIVE, MS1959 DRIVE, GUITAR GRAPHIC EQ7, CLONE CHORUS and HALL REVERB, with their default values.
    2. Note the DSP load the builder shows (about 68.2%), then download the .zptc file.
    3. Load it on the pedal as in test 1, into a free memory.
    4. On the pedal step through the effects of the patch, from the first to the last.

    Send us the answers to these questions

    • After the copy, how many effects does the app show for the patch, and which ones?
    • How many effects can you reach on the pedal? Is the sixth, HALL REVERB, there?
    • Which processor or DSP percentage does the app show?
    • Did the app or the pedal show any message?

    Test 4: parametric EQ

    About 15 minutes. The builder cannot write the FREQ and Q of the PARAMETRIC EQ yet, because we do not know how the pedal stores them. On the MS-60B+, which has the same ranges, Q grows in steps of 0.1 from 0.5 (checked up to 3.2) and FREQ does not grow in even steps. Here we need to see whether the MS-50G+ does the same.

    1. In the app make six patches in free memories. Each one holds a single PARAMETRIC EQ (leave GAIN and VOL as they are) with the FREQ and Q of the table below.
    2. Name each patch after its values, as the app shows them, for example PEQ 50Hz 3.2. If the app cannot show exactly a value of the table, take the nearest one and name the patch after what it really shows.
    3. Select the six memories, tap Share and send the files to yourself.

    Test 7: amp models

    About 3 minutes per amp. Amps carry data that ZOOM does not document: on the MS-60B+ every amp has a hidden ninth value and a flag of its own in the patch header. We want to know whether the MS-50G+ does the same.

    Make a patch with only that amp, leave its values as they are and name the patch after the amp. Select the memories, share them and send the files. Start with MS1959 DRIVE, FD TWIN-R DRIVE and RECTI DUAL DRIVE (about 10 minutes); the more amps the better.

    The 22 amp models: MS45OS DRIVE, MS1959 DRIVE, MS800 DRIVE, FD B-MAN DRIVE, FD TWIN-R DRIVE, FD DELUXE-R DRIVE, FD MASTER DRIVE, UK 30A DRIVE, BG MARK1 DRIVE, BG MARK3 DRIVE, RECTI DUAL DRIVE, XTASY BLUE DRIVE, HW 100 DRIVE, ORG120 DRIVE, DZ DRIVE, MATCH30 DRIVE, KRAMPUS DRIVE, REDLOOM DRIVE, VELVET DRIVE, MUDDY DRIVE, 7 HEAVEN DRIVE, POLLEX DRIVE.

    Test 8: add-on effects

    About 3 minutes per effect. The effects marked with a star in the ZOOM Effects List are added to the pedal with Handy Guitar Lab, and the builder does not know their identifiers yet, so it cannot use them. If you have added some, make a patch with only that effect, leave its values as they are, name the patch after the effect, share it and send the file.

    The 24 add-on effects: OPTICAL COMPRESSOR, MUTE SWITCH, RESONANCE FILTER, CRY FILTER, LFO FILTER, RANDOM FILTER, GUITAR GRAPHIC EQ, STEREO GUITAR GRAPHIC EQ, EXCITER, LOW EQ, HIGH EQ, BG GRID DRIVE, METAL WORLD DRIVE, TS+BOOST DRIVE, RED CRUNCH DRIVE + BOOST, SPOT BOOSTER, UP OCTAVE BOOSTER, OUTPUT BOOSTER, CHORUS, BEND CHORUS, DUO PHASER, PHASE DELAY, AUTO PAN DELAY, SLOW ATTACK DELAY.

    Send us the results

    Write to us with everything in one message: the answers of each test in the body (a line per question is enough), and the files attached. Say which tests you did, in any order. Please include the firmware version of your pedal, the version of the Handy Guitar Lab app and the model and system version of your iPhone or iPad.

    We use the answers to correct the builder and, when the checks pass, to mark the MS-50G+ as tested on your firmware version. A patch file holds only the patch: its effects, their values and the name you gave it.
